Chapter 01 · The past

Used to

used to+base verb

Past habits or states that are no longer true.

Past only! There is no present form. For today's habits we need another word…
Past habit
I used to play football every Saturday.
…but I don't anymore.
Past state
She used to live in London.
Now she lives somewhere else.
Negative · no “d”
I didn't use to like coffee.
Question · no “d”
Did you use to have long hair?
Chapter 02 · It's normal for me

Be used to

Something is normal and familiar for you. You're accustomed to it.

be used to+noun/verb-ing
+ verb-ing
I'm used to getting up early.
It's no problem for me.
+ noun · negative
He isn't used to the noise of the city.
Any tense — “be” changes
In Spain, I was used to eating dinner late.
Here “to” is a preposition — so the verb takes -ing, never the base form.
Chapter 03 · The process

Get used to

Something is new or strange, and little by little it becomes normal.

get used to+noun/verb-ing
Happening now
I'm getting used to driving on the left.
Future · + noun
Don't worry — you'll get used to the weather.
Past
It took me months to get used to living alone.
Chapter 04 · The present

Usually

An adverb of frequency for present habits — with the present simple.

usually+present simple
Before the main verb
I usually take the bus to work.
After the verb “to be”
She's usually on time.
Classic mistake
I use to go to the gym.
I usually go to the gym.
Chapter 05 · Side by side

Same verb. Four stories.

Used to
I used to work at night.
A past habit. It's finished now.
Be used to
I'm used to working at night.
It's normal for me. No problem.
Get used to
I'm getting used to working at night.
It's becoming normal, step by step.
Usually
I usually work at night.
A regular habit in the present.
Same verb — four different meanings!
